The Core Problem

Airports Are a System
of Systems

Dozens of organisations — each with its own mandate, technology portfolio, and governance structure — must collaborate in near-real time to deliver a consistent passenger experience. The architect who arrives with a conventional enterprise mental model will produce designs that are technically correct but operationally insufficient.

This handbook exists to close that gap. It translates thirty-five years of field experience into structured, applicable knowledge — from AODB event modelling to post-quantum cryptography readiness, from biometric pipeline design to tender response methodology.

Topic 4 — Flagship Chapter

AODB — The Central Nervous System

The Airport Operational Database is the authoritative system of record for all planned and actual flight movements. It is the single source of truth from which every connected system derives its operational picture.


Designing an AODB is a governance exercise as much as a database exercise: who owns which data, which system is authoritative, and how update authority is controlled under disruption.

Topics 13–14

Integration Architecture & API Strategy

The choice of integration pattern is not a technical decision — it is an organisational and operational one. SOA and EDA are not alternatives; they serve different interaction patterns and must be deployed together in every mature airport environment.

"The integration decision you make in the baggage hall has consequences in the AOCC."

SOA — synchronous, explicit contracts, organisational alignment enforced at the interface
EDA + Kafka — decoupled, resilient, scales with operational event volume; idempotent producers, dead-letter queues
Event Sourcing + CQRS — immutable log with three read projections: current state, analytics, audit trail
Protocol taxonomy — REST / WebSocket / MQTT / gRPC matched to four latency bands for airport operations
Interface versioning — breaking vs. non-breaking changes, sunset windows, migration contracts

Topics 18–22

Zero Trust & OT Cybersecurity

Airport cybersecurity spans IT and OT systems, shared-use infrastructure operated by multiple organisations, and safety-critical systems. Zero Trust Architecture (NIST SP 800-207) — assume breach, verify explicitly, apply least privilege — is the only model that fits this environment.

Zero Trust PDP/PEP model — policy decision and enforcement points; least-privilege per request
Micro-segmentation — IT/OT network isolation, Purdue model enforcement at the OT boundary
Continuous threat detection — SIEM, SOAR, anomaly baselines on ramp & terminal OT systems
GDPR Article 9 — biometric data requires explicit consent; Art. 6 lawful bases are not interchangeable
Post-quantum readiness — NIST PQC standards, crypto-agility architecture, cryptographic inventory
